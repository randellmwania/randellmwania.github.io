Problem & Context

Coffee represents one of East Africa's leading agricultural exports and economic backbones. However, fungal and bacterial foliar diseases cause severe crop losses before symptoms are visibly noticeable to the naked eye. Traditional scouting requires scarce agronomic expertise across vast plantation hectarage. This project bridges deep learning computer vision directly into smallholder crop management.

Architecture & Pipeline

The machine learning solution integrates an end-to-end computer vision pipeline developed in Python and TensorFlow:

  • Image Preprocessing & Color Space Conversion: Images are normalized and augmented through random spatial flipping, rotational jitter, and contrast normalization to reflect unpredictable field lighting.
  • Feature Extraction: Sequential 2D convolutional blocks extract hierarchical spatial representations, transitioning from low-level leaf venation edges to high-level necrotic lesion patterns.
  • Batch Normalization & Dropout: Integrated between convolutional blocks to stabilize gradient descent and mitigate overfitting on background soil/vegetation artifacts.
  • Softmax Classification: Outputs probabilistic confidence scores across disease categories, flagging suspected infections when confidence exceeds threshold markers.

Impact & Results

The resulting model achieved a robust 90% accuracy score on unseen test evaluations. By empowering farm managers to localize infection hot-spots days earlier than traditional manual scouting, targeted fungicide and cultural treatments can be applied with precision, reducing chemical runoff and protecting crop yields.
